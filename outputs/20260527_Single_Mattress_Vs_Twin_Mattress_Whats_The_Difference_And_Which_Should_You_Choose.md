## Single vs Twin vs Other Sizes: Where It Fits in the Full Picture

Think of mattress sizes as a ladder. Single/twin sits at the first rung — compact, affordable, and purpose-built for one person.

**Single vs Double:** A double is 12 inches wider. If you move around a lot in sleep, or occasionally share the bed, that extra width makes a real difference. For a child's room or a hostel setup, though, a single is perfectly sufficient — and much easier to fit into tighter spaces. The wider surface area of a double also allows for better pressure relief across different sleeping positions throughout the night.

**Single vs Queen:** A queen is 24 inches wider and 6 inches longer. These two aren't really competing — a queen is a completely different category, built for couples or adults who want a genuinely spacious solo sleep experience. Comparing these two means you've probably already outgrown the single.

**Single vs Single XL:** This is the comparison that actually matters for taller buyers. The XL adds 6 inches in length (78" vs 72") while keeping the same 36" width. Above 5'9"? The XL is worth every extra rupee — your feet will thank you, and your spine won't spend the night in a compromised position.

## Who Should Buy a Single/Twin Mattress? (And Who Shouldn't)

### ✅ Great fit for:

- **Kids (ages 5–14):** Fits most children's bedrooms comfortably. Go firm for growing spines — more on that below. Proper spinal alignment during childhood is genuinely important, not just marketing language.
- **Teenagers and college students:** Single beds are the default in hostel rooms and PG accommodations. Lightweight, easy to move, easy to set up.
- **Guest rooms:** Compact, cost-effective, and does the job without taking over the room.
- **Adults in studio apartments:** When every square foot counts, a single frees up significant floor space.
- **People who move homes frequently:** Easier to transport, shift, and set up than larger sizes.

### ❌ Not the best fit for:

- **Couples:** Even if you're cozy people, 36 inches of width is tight for two. A double is the minimum.
- **Adults above 5'10":** You'll likely hang off the edge. Look at Single XL or Double.
- **Active sleepers:** If you toss and turn through the night, you'll genuinely appreciate the extra width of a double.
- **People with chronic back pain who need space to shift positions:** More surface area means better pressure distribution and pressure relief across the night.

## How to Pick the Right Single Mattress: A No-Nonsense Buyer's Guide

Use this as your checklist before you add to cart:

**1. Confirm the exact dimensions first.**
Always check the measurements on the product page. "Single" and "twin" usually mean 72" × 36" in India, but some international brands list 75" × 38". Measure your bed frame if you already have one — a 2-inch mismatch is more annoying than it sounds.

**2. Match firmness to the sleeper's age and weight.**
- Kids & teens → Firm to Medium Firm (spinal alignment is the priority)
- Lightweight adults (under 60kg) → Medium to Medium Soft
- Average adults (60–90kg) → Medium to Firm
- Heavier adults (90kg+) → Firm to Very Firm

**3. Think about the room's purpose.**
Guest room = comfort and pressure relief focused. Kids' room = support and spinal alignment focused. Hostel/PG = durability + portability. Each has a different priority, and the right mattress for one setup is often the wrong choice for another.

**4. Don't ignore breathability.**
India gets hot. If the sleeper runs warm, look for open-cell foam (AirGen™) or latex with Pinhole Tech — both allow better airflow and breathability than traditional foam. The Ultima's CoolTec™ cover actively draws heat away from the surface.

**5. Factor in portability if the room demands it.**
If the mattress needs to be stored during the day or moved between rooms, a foldable option like the Tri Fold is a genuinely smart call — not a compromise.

**6. Check the trial period and warranty.**
A good mattress should come with at least a 100-night trial so you can test it at home in real conditions. All SleepyCat mattresses come with a 100-night trial and a 10-year warranty (the Tri Fold carries a 1-year warranty on foam and stitching).
